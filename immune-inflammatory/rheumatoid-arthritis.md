# Rheumatoid Arthritis

<p class="mh-subtitle">ATI-focused review of systemic autoimmune inflammation, joint destruction, bony ankylosis, laboratory findings, pain and function, disease-modifying therapy, medication safety, and patient education</p>

**Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** is a chronic **systemic autoimmune disease** that causes inflammation of the synovial joints and may also affect muscles and other body systems. Without effective treatment, repeated inflammation can destroy the joint and progress to **bony ankylosis**, causing permanent loss of movement and function.

### RA Basics & Disease Progression

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>What Rheumatoid Arthritis Is</strong>

- Chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease
- Systemic rather than limited to one joint
- Body attacks its own synovial tissue
- May involve joints, muscles, and other systems
- Often begins before older adulthood
- Juvenile forms can occur in children
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>What “Systemic” Means</strong>

- Inflammation is not limited to local wear and tear.
- Multiple joints may be affected.
- Fatigue and malaise may occur.
- Inflammatory blood tests may be elevated.
- Muscles and other organs can be involved.
- Treatment must control the immune response.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Instructor Emphasis</strong>

- RA is classified as autoimmune.
- Mycoplasma has been cultured from synovial fluid in some discussions.
- The course notes mention tetracycline-family treatment in early RA.
- The major test outcome is untreated progression to **bony ankylosis**.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>RA Disease Process:</strong> Autoimmune inflammation attacks the synovium → the joint becomes warm, swollen, painful, and stiff → repeated inflammation damages cartilage and bone → the joint enlarges and deforms → bones may grow together → movement and function are lost.
</div>

<div class="detail-table">
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr>
        <th>Disease Step</th>
        <th>What Happens</th>
        <th>Clinical Result</th>
      </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr>
        <td><strong>Synovial inflammation</strong></td>
        <td>The immune system attacks the tissue lining the joint.</td>
        <td>Pain, warmth, swelling, and stiffness develop.</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td><strong>Persistent inflammation</strong></td>

      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td><strong>Joint destruction</strong></td>
        <td>Cartilage and bone are progressively damaged.</td>
        <td>Deformity and loss of function occur.</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td><strong>Bony ankylosis</strong></td>
        <td>The bones grow together and the normal joint space disappears.</td>
        <td>The joint can no longer move.</td>
      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Bony Ankylosis</strong>

- End-stage joint destruction in the course notes
- Joint first becomes enlarged
- Normal joint structure disappears
- Bones come together or grow together
- Permanent loss of movement develops
- Prevention requires early disease control
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Flare</strong>

- Increased pain
- Increased swelling
- Increased warmth
- Increased stiffness
- Fatigue may worsen
- Rest needs increase
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Remission</strong>

- Symptoms decrease.
- Inflammation becomes less active.
- Function may improve.
- Medication continues as prescribed.
- A lack of symptoms does not mean the disease is cured.
</div>

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
RA treatment is not only about pain relief. The long-term priority is preventing irreversible joint destruction and preserving function.
</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>RA = Rheumatic Attack:</strong> The immune system attacks the joint, and untreated inflammation can remove the joint through bony ankylosis.
</div>

</div>

### Assessment & Manifestations

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Classic Joint Pattern</strong>

- Symmetric involvement
- Same joints affected on both sides
- Small joints of hands and feet are common
- Wrists may be involved
- Multiple joints may be affected
- Joint findings are inflammatory
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Inflammatory Joint Findings</strong>

- Warmth
- Swelling
- Tenderness
- Pain
- Decreased movement
- Prolonged morning stiffness
- Pain after inactivity
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Systemic Findings</strong>

- Fatigue
- Malaise
- Weakness
- Low-grade fever during inflammation
- Reduced appetite
- Weight changes
- Muscle involvement
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Common Functional Problems</strong>

- Opening jars
- Buttoning clothing
- Gripping utensils
- Writing
- Walking
- Climbing stairs
- Performing hygiene
- Completing household tasks
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Later Joint Changes</strong>

- Enlarged joints
- Contractures
- Reduced grip strength
- Finger deformities
- Loss of alignment
- Limited movement
- Bony ankylosis
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Psychosocial Effects</strong>

- Frustration
- Role changes
- Reduced independence
- Chronic-pain fatigue
- Depression or anxiety
- Altered body image
- Work and financial concerns
</div>

</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- Fever while taking a DMARD, biologic, steroid, or immunosuppressant
- Sudden shortness of breath or edema
- New chest pain
- Unusual bleeding or bruising
- Severe weakness
- Abrupt decrease in urine output
- Severe medication rash
</div>

### Diagnostics & Laboratory Monitoring

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>ESR</strong>

- Erythrocyte sedimentation rate
- Increases with inflammation
- Does not identify the exact cause or location
- May be elevated in RA
- May be used to follow disease activity
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>C-Reactive Protein</strong>

- Another marker of inflammation
- May rise with active RA
- Does not diagnose RA alone
- Trends may help evaluate treatment response
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Rheumatoid Factor</strong>

- Present in many clients with RA
- Supports the diagnosis
- Does not determine severity by itself
- Must be interpreted with symptoms and other findings
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Before Immunosuppressive Therapy</strong>

- Baseline CBC
- Liver-function tests
- Kidney-function tests
- Infection assessment
- Pregnancy assessment when relevant
- Tuberculosis testing before TNF blockers
- Hepatitis screening as ordered
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>During Therapy</strong>

- Repeat CBC
- Repeat liver tests
- Monitor urine output
- Assess for infection
- Assess for bleeding
- Monitor medication effectiveness
- Review adverse effects
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Laboratory Warning Signs</strong>

- Falling white blood cell count
- Falling platelet count
- Worsening anemia
- Elevated liver enzymes
- Rising creatinine
- Abrupt urine-output decrease
- Signs of infection with low counts
</div>

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
ESR and CRP show that inflammation is present. They do not tell the nurse exactly where the inflammation is or prove RA by themselves.
</div>

### Comfort, Mobility & Functional Nursing Care

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Main Nursing Goals</strong>

- Promote independence.
- Maintain function.
- Improve comfort.
- Prevent deformity.
- Protect joints.
- Balance activity and rest.
- Support long-term self-management.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Heat and Ice</strong>

- The client may prefer one or both.
- Apply for **20 minutes**.
- Remove for **20–40 minutes**.
- Do not place directly on skin.
- Check the skin before and after.
- Avoid when sensation is impaired.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Range of Motion</strong>

- Perform **3–4 times/day**.
- Active ROM is best when possible.
- Move slowly and gently.
- Never move beyond pain.
- Never force beyond resistance.
- Continue consistently to preserve movement.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Joint Protection</strong>

- Use larger and stronger joints.
- Avoid prolonged gripping.
- Avoid carrying heavy items with the fingers.
- Use both hands when possible.
- Maintain proper alignment.
- Avoid repetitive stress.
- Use assistive devices.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Energy Conservation</strong>

- Plan activities.
- Prioritize essential tasks.
- Sit for care when possible.
- Rest before becoming exhausted.
- Keep supplies within reach.
- Ask for help with heavy tasks.
- Schedule activity during best-energy periods.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Adaptive Equipment</strong>

- Built-up utensil handles
- Jar-opening devices
- Button hooks
- Reachers
- Raised toilet seat
- Shower chair
- Mobility aids
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Activity Balance:</strong> Too little movement increases stiffness and contracture risk. Too much activity during inflammation increases pain and fatigue. The goal is regular gentle movement with planned rest.
</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Exercise Teaching</strong>

- Choose low-impact exercise.
- Warm joints before activity.
- Stop before severe fatigue.
- Avoid exercising an acutely inflamed joint aggressively.
- Follow physical and occupational therapy plans.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Home Safety</strong>

- Remove fall hazards.
- Use nonslip surfaces.
- Install grab bars when needed.
- Wear supportive shoes.
- Keep frequently used items accessible.
- Protect painful joints from injury.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Comfort Teaching</strong>

- Take pain medication before planned activity when prescribed.
- Use supportive positioning.
- Maintain good sleep habits.
- Report uncontrolled pain.
- Do not use extreme heat or cold.
- Protect numb skin.
</div>

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
The best intervention is usually the one that preserves independence without forcing a painful or damaged joint.
</div>

</div>

### Medications & DMARD Safety

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>NSAIDs</strong>

- Often used before acetaminophen because RA requires anti-inflammatory action.
- Inhibit prostaglandin synthesis.
- Reduce pain and inflammation.
- Do not stop disease progression.
- Monitor GI bleeding and kidney function.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Corticosteroids</strong>

- Used when NSAIDs do not provide enough relief.
- Examples include prednisone, prednisolone, and hydrocortisone.
- Rapidly suppress inflammation.
- Do not stop suddenly after long-term use.
- Increase infection and glucose risks.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Topical Analgesics</strong>

- Capsaicin may be used for local discomfort.
- Apply with gloves.
- Avoid broken skin.
- Wash hands after use.
- Burning may occur initially.
- Avoid contact with eyes.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Why DMARDs Matter:</strong> NSAIDs and steroids reduce symptoms. Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs interrupt the immune response and help prevent continued joint destruction.
</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Methotrexate Priority Teaching</strong>

- Take exactly as prescribed.
- Monitor CBC.
- Monitor liver-function tests.
- Avoid alcohol.
- Report fever.
- Report diarrhea.
- Report bleeding or bruising.
- Report mouth sores.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Biologic Priority Teaching</strong>

- Test for tuberculosis before therapy.
- Screen for hepatitis B as ordered.
- Report fever or infection.
- Avoid sick contacts.
- Learn subcutaneous injection technique when applicable.
- Monitor for fluid-volume excess or heart-failure symptoms.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Hydroxychloroquine Priority Teaching</strong>

- Keep baseline and routine eye examinations.
- Report visual changes.
- Do not stop therapy independently.
- Take as prescribed for long-term disease control.
- Benefit may take time.
</div>

</div>

<strong>Report Infection</strong>

- Fever
- Chills
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Painful urination
- Draining wound
- New fatigue
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Report Bleeding</strong>

- Easy bruising
- Petechiae
- Bleeding gums
- Black stool
- Blood in urine
- Prolonged bleeding
- New weakness or dizziness
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Report Organ Toxicity</strong>

- Jaundice
- Dark urine
- Abrupt decrease in urine output
- Edema
- Shortness of breath
- Severe fatigue
- Visual changes
</div>

</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Hold and Report</h3>

Fever, serious infection, significant decrease in white cells or platelets, severe liver abnormalities, abrupt decrease in urine output, or new heart-failure findings require prompt provider notification.
</div>

<div class="detail-table">
  <table>

      <tr>

| Concept | Main Point | Priority |

| **Synovitis** | Immune inflammation of the joint lining | Reduce pain, swelling, and damage |
| **Bony ankylosis** | Bones grow together and joint movement is lost | Prevent with early disease control and mobility care |
| **ESR and CRP** | General inflammation markers | Trend disease activity and response |
| **Rheumatoid factor** | Present in many RA cases | Interpret with symptoms and other findings |
| **DMARD** | Slows immune-mediated joint destruction | Adherence and toxicity monitoring are essential |

| **Biologics** | Block specific inflammatory pathways | Screen for TB and serious infection |
| **ROM** | Maintains movement and function | Active ROM 3–4 times/day without forcing |
| **Heat and ice** | Improve comfort | 20 minutes on and 20–40 minutes off |

<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- Fever or infection while immunosuppressed
- Unusual bleeding or bruising
- Severe mouth sores
- Jaundice or dark urine
- Abrupt decrease in urine output
- New shortness of breath or edema
- Severe medication rash

<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Quick Review</h3>

1. Rheumatoid arthritis is a systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease.
2. RA often affects the same joints on both sides of the body.
3. Prolonged morning stiffness supports an inflammatory pattern.
4. Untreated RA can progress to bony ankylosis and complete loss of joint movement.
5. ESR and CRP indicate inflammation but do not diagnose RA alone.
6. Rheumatoid factor is present in many cases.
7. The nursing goals are independence, function, and comfort.
8. Apply heat or ice for 20 minutes and remove it for 20–40 minutes.
9. Perform active ROM 3–4 times/day and never force beyond pain or resistance.
10. NSAIDs reduce inflammation but do not stop disease progression.
11. DMARDs slow immune-mediated joint destruction.
12. Methotrexate can cause bone-marrow suppression and hepatotoxicity.
13. Biologics increase serious infection and TB risk.
14. Hydroxychloroquine requires regular eye examinations.
</div>

<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Do Not Miss</h3>

- Do not confuse RA with local wear-and-tear osteoarthritis.
- Do not force an inflamed joint past pain or resistance.
- Do not keep a client completely immobile between flares.
- Do not apply heat or ice directly to the skin.
- Do not use heat or ice carelessly when sensation is impaired.
- Do not ignore fever in a client taking a DMARD or biologic.
- Do not ignore bleeding, mouth sores, or liver findings with methotrexate.
- Do not begin a TNF blocker without required infection screening.
- Do not stop long-term corticosteroids suddenly.
- Do not overlook the long-term goal of preventing joint destruction.
</div>

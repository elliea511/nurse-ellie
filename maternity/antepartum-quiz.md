## Question 1

<p>A nurse is teaching a pregnant client about normal cardiovascular changes. Which finding should the nurse identify as an expected adaptation to pregnancy?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> A persistent increase in blood pressure</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> A functional systolic murmur</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> A decrease in cardiac output</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> A decrease in circulating blood volume</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: B</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Blood pressure normally does not rise during an uncomplicated pregnancy and often decreases slightly during midpregnancy.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Correct. Increased blood volume, cardiac output, and peripheral vasodilation can produce an audible functional or innocent systolic murmur.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. Cardiac output increases during pregnancy.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Maternal blood volume increases by approximately 40% to 50%.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Question 2

<p>A pregnant client at 30 weeks becomes lightheaded and reports palpitations while lying flat for an examination. Which action should the nurse take first?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> Place the client in a side-lying position</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Apply oxygen by nonrebreather mask</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> Obtain a blood glucose level</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Assist the client to stand and walk</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: A</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Correct. The enlarged uterus can compress the inferior vena cava in the supine position. Turning the client to a lateral position, preferably left side-lying, relieves the compression and improves venous return.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Incorrect. Oxygen is not the first action when the cause is positional vena cava compression.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. Hypoglycemia is not the most likely cause of symptoms that begin while supine late in pregnancy.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Standing could worsen hypotension and increase fall risk.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Question 3

<p>Which laboratory pattern best explains physiologic anemia of pregnancy?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> A greater increase in red blood cells than plasma volume</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Destruction of red blood cells by fetal antibodies</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> A greater increase in plasma volume than red blood cell mass</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Failure of the bone marrow to produce red blood cells</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: C</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Plasma volume increases more than red blood cell mass.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Incorrect. Antibody-mediated hemolysis is pathologic and is not the cause of physiologic anemia.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Correct. Plasma volume rises approximately 40% to 50%, while red blood cell mass rises less, causing hemodilution and lower maternal hemoglobin and hematocrit.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Maternal bone marrow production increases rather than fails.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Question 5

<p>Which findings are expected cardiovascular or hematologic adaptations to pregnancy?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> Increased circulating fibrinogen</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Increased risk for venous thromboembolism</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> Increased heart rate by approximately 15 to 20 beats/min</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Decreased blood volume</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>E.</strong> Varicosities of the legs, vulva, or rectum</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: A, B, C, E</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Correct. Clotting factors, including fibrinogen, increase during the second and third trimesters.</p>

<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Blood volume increases.</p>
<p><strong>E.</strong> Correct. Venous compression and decreased venous return can cause leg or vulvar varicosities and hemorrhoids.</p>

## Question 12

<p>A pregnant client reports increased white vaginal discharge without odor, itching, or pain. Which response by the nurse is best?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> “This is expected leukorrhea; use cotton underwear and avoid douching.”</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> “This finding confirms rupture of membranes.”</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> “Begin using a vaginal douche twice daily.”</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> “This finding means you have a sexually transmitted infection.”</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: A</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Correct. Leukorrhea is common in pregnancy. Daily hygiene, cotton underwear, front-to-back wiping, and avoiding douching are appropriate.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Incorrect. Rupture of membranes usually causes watery fluid leakage, not typical white leukorrhea.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. Douching can alter normal flora and is not recommended unless specifically prescribed.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Infection is more likely when discharge is foul-smelling, colored, irritating, or associated with pain.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Question 1

<p>Which period is known as the puerperium?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> The first trimester of pregnancy</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> The 6 weeks following childbirth</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> The period from complete dilation to birth</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> The first year after childbirth</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: B</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. This is part of the antepartum period.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Correct. The puerperium is the approximately 6-week period after childbirth when maternal systems return toward the prepregnant state.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. This is the second stage of labor.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Maternal adaptation continues beyond 6 weeks, but the puerperium specifically refers to the early 6-week period.</p>
</div>
</details>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> The initiation of breast milk production</p>

<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: C</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Prolactin initiates milk production.</p>

## Question 3

<p>Which physiologic process normally prevents excessive bleeding from the placental site after birth?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> Relaxation of the uterine muscle</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Contraction and retraction of uterine muscle fibers</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Increased bladder capacity</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: B</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Uterine relaxation increases hemorrhage risk.</p>

## Question 4

<p>Which postpartum cardiovascular finding is expected?</p>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> Cardiac output immediately falls below prepregnancy levels</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Cardiac output remains elevated for about 48 hours</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> Blood volume doubles after birth</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Clotting factors immediately return to normal</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: B</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Cardiac output initially rises because of autotransfusion from the contracting uterus and relief of vena cava compression.</p>

<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. Maternal blood volume does not double.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Clotting factors remain elevated for several weeks, contributing to thrombosis risk.</p>
</div>

<p class="answer-choice"><strong>A.</strong> Focus only on newborn care</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>B.</strong> Detect hemorrhage, infection, thrombosis, hypertension, and mental health emergencies early</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>C.</strong> Delay teaching until the 6-week visit</p>
<p class="answer-choice"><strong>D.</strong> Assume postpartum symptoms are normal unless severe</p>

<details class="quiz-answer-block">
<summary>Click to see answer and rationale</summary>
<p class="quiz-answer"><strong>Answer: B</strong></p>
<div class="quiz-rationale">
<p><strong>Rationale:</strong></p>
<p><strong>A.</strong> Incorrect. Maternal and newborn care are both essential.</p>
<p><strong>B.</strong> Correct. Postpartum nursing centers on early recognition of life-threatening physical and mental health complications while supporting recovery and parenting.</p>
<p><strong>C.</strong> Incorrect. Teaching must begin before discharge.</p>
<p><strong>D.</strong> Incorrect. Subtle findings can signal serious complications.</p>
</div>
</details>

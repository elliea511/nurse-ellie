## NCLEX Quick Cues: Sensory Perception

<div class="priority-focus">
  <div>
    <h3>Think First</h3>
    Sudden Vision Loss • Severe Eye Pain • Intraocular Pressure • Retinal Detachment • Fall Risk • Ear Surgery Safety • Medication Technique • Injury Prevention
  </div>
</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Vision Cue</strong>

- Sudden vision loss is always urgent.
- Severe eye pain with nausea and halos suggests acute angle closure.
- Flashes, floaters, or a curtain suggest retinal detachment.
- Cataracts cause painless gradual blurred vision.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Vision-Loss Pattern</strong>

- Glaucoma causes peripheral-vision loss.
- Macular degeneration causes central-vision loss.
- Retinal detachment causes a curtain or shadow.
- Cataracts cause cloudy, blurred, glare-sensitive vision.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Hearing & Balance Cue</strong>

- Vertigo creates immediate fall risk.
- Ménière’s disease causes vertigo, tinnitus, and fluctuating hearing loss.
- Stapedectomy clients may have temporary balance problems.
- Sudden hearing loss requires prompt evaluation.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-table">

| When You See This | Think This | Best Nursing Move |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe eye pain + headache + nausea + halos** | Acute angle-closure glaucoma | Treat as an emergency and notify provider immediately |
| **Gradual painless blurred vision + glare** | Cataract | Promote safety and prepare for planned treatment |
| **Severe pain after cataract surgery** | Increased IOP or complication | Report immediately |
| **Flashes + floaters + curtain over vision** | Retinal detachment | Limit eye movement as ordered and prepare for urgent repair |
| **Peripheral-vision loss** | Glaucoma | Reinforce lifelong pressure-control therapy |
| **Central-vision loss** | Macular degeneration | Use magnification, strong lighting, and home-safety measures |
| **Eye drops ordered** | Conjunctival-sac administration | Pull lower lid down, instill into sac, and apply inner-canthus pressure |
| **Client bends after eye surgery** | Increased intraocular pressure risk | Stop the activity and reinforce restrictions |
| **Vertigo + tinnitus + hearing changes** | Ménière’s disease | Sit or lie down, reduce stimulation, and prevent falls |
| **Balance problems after stapedectomy** | Expected temporary postoperative effect | Assist with ambulation and use fall precautions |
| **Adult ear drops** | Straighten adult canal | Pull pinna up and back |
| **Infant or toddler ear drops** | Straighten young-child canal | Pull pinna down and back |
| **Bean or peanut in ear** | Absorbent foreign body | Do not irrigate |
| **Bloody ear drainage after trauma** | Possible skull or tympanic injury | Do not occlude and report immediately |

</div>

<div class="mh-columns" markdown="1">

<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Priority Order</h3>

1. Sudden vision loss or acute eye pain.
2. Airway, neurologic injury, or trauma-related drainage.
3. Fall prevention during vertigo or after ear surgery.
4. Prevent increased intraocular pressure after eye surgery.
5. Correct medication administration.
6. Long-term adaptation for chronic vision or hearing loss.
</div>

<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Fast Traps</h3>

- Do not ignore severe pain after cataract extraction.
- Do not stop glaucoma drops when symptoms are absent.
- Do not bend, strain, cough forcefully, or lift heavily after eye surgery.
- Do not place eye drops directly on the cornea.
- Do not confuse glaucoma peripheral loss with macular central loss.
- Do not irrigate beans or peanuts in the ear.
- Do not ambulate a client with active vertigo without assistance.
- Do not use dry cotton after ear drops.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Sensory NCLEX Rule:</strong> Sudden changes, severe pain, pressure, retinal symptoms, and fall risk always outrank routine teaching.
</div>

# Renal Diagnostics & Catheter Care

<p class="mh-subtitle">ATI-focused review of kidney function, clean-catch urine, renal laboratory values, IVP, cystoscopy, 24-hour urine collection, sterile catheter insertion, and catheter specimen collection</p>

The kidneys filter the blood, remove wastes and excess water, regulate electrolytes and acid-base balance, and produce erythropoietin. Diagnostic questions usually test **specimen technique, exact laboratory values, contrast safety, expected versus unexpected findings, sterile catheter care, and urine output**.

### Kidney Function Basics

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Filtration</strong>

- Blood enters the kidneys through the renal arteries.
- The glomeruli filter water and dissolved substances from the blood.
- Tubules reabsorb substances the body needs.
- Remaining wastes and excess fluid become urine.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Waste Removal</strong>

- Creatinine
- Nitrogen waste measured by BUN
- Hydrogen ions
- Potassium
- Phosphorus
- Excess water
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Endocrine Function</strong>

- Kidneys produce erythropoietin.
- Erythropoietin stimulates red blood cell production.
- Kidney failure causes anemia because erythropoietin decreases.
- Dialysis does not replace erythropoietin production.
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Healthy Kidney Function | Finding When Function Fails |
|---|---|
| Removes hydrogen ions | Metabolic acidosis |
| Removes potassium | Hyperkalemia and dysrhythmias |
| Removes phosphorus | Hyperphosphatemia |
| Supports calcium balance | Hypocalcemia |
| Removes water | Edema and hypertension |
| Removes nitrogen wastes | Elevated BUN and creatinine |
| Produces erythropoietin | Anemia |

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Kidneys Filter, Balance, and Build Blood:</strong> They remove wastes, control fluid and electrolytes, regulate acid-base balance, and produce erythropoietin.
</div>
</div>

### Urine Specimens

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Clean-Catch Basics</strong>

- Clean the urinary meatus.
- Begin voiding into the toilet.
- Collect urine in midstream.
- Avoid touching the inside of the cup or lid.
- Finish voiding after the specimen is collected.
- Send promptly to the laboratory.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Female Cleansing</strong>

- Separate the labia.
- Keep the labia separated.
- Use one cleansing wipe down one side.
- Use a second wipe down the other side.
- Use a third wipe down the center.
- Wipe from front to back.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Male Cleansing</strong>

- Retract the foreskin if present.
- Clean from the urinary meatus outward in a circular motion.
- Begin voiding before collecting.
- Return the foreskin after collection.
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Clean Catch = Clean, Start, Catch:</strong> Clean the meatus, start urinating, then catch the midstream urine.
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| 24-Hour Urine Step | Correct Technique |
|---|---|
| Start time | Void and discard the first urine |
| Collection period | Save every urine specimen for the next 24 hours |
| End time | Include the final void at the exact ending time |
| Storage | Keep chilled or on ice when ordered |
| Missed specimen | Notify the nurse or laboratory because the test may need to restart |

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
A single missed urine specimen can invalidate a 24-hour collection.
</div>
</div>

### Renal Laboratory Values

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Test | Course Value | Main Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| **BUN** | **10–20 mg/dL** | Nitrogen waste; may rise with kidney dysfunction and other conditions |
| **Serum creatinine** | **0.6–1.2 mg/dL** | Major routine indicator of kidney filtration |
| **Urine specific gravity** | **1.015–1.025** | Concentration of urine in the instructor renal notes |
| **Minimum urine output** | **30 mL/hr** | Minimum adequate output |
| **Desired postoperative urine output** | **60 mL/hr** | Course target after urinary surgery |

</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>BUN</strong>

- Measures nitrogen waste.
- May rise with renal dysfunction.
- Can also be affected by other conditions.
- Interpret with creatinine and clinical findings.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Creatinine</strong>

- Reflects kidney filtration.
- Rising creatinine suggests worsening renal function.
- Trend is more useful than one isolated value.
- Important before contrast or nephrotoxic medication.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Urine Output</strong>

- Less than 30 mL/hr is urgent.
- Assess perfusion, obstruction, tubing, and kidney function.
- Compare output with intake.
- Report abrupt changes.
</div>
</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- Urine output below 30 mL/hr
- Abrupt decrease from baseline
- Rising creatinine
- New hyperkalemia
- No urine with bladder or flank pain
- New edema or respiratory distress
</div>
</div>

### Intravenous Pyelogram & Cystoscopy

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Intravenous Pyelogram</strong>

- IV iodine-based dye outlines the kidneys and renal pelvis.
- X-rays show urinary structures.
- Assess renal function before the test.
- Ask about iodine or shellfish allergy in the course notes.
- Contraindicated in pregnancy.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Metformin Safety</strong>

- Hold metformin **48 hours before** the IVP.
- Hold metformin **48 hours after** the IVP.
- Contrast can worsen kidney function.
- Metformin accumulation may contribute to metabolic acidosis.
- Restart only as instructed.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>After Contrast</strong>

- Encourage fluids when not contraindicated.
- Monitor urine output.
- Monitor renal function.
- Watch for allergic reaction.
- Report breathing difficulty or facial swelling.
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Cystoscopy | Priority Point |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Scope inserted through the urethra into the bladder |
| Anesthesia | Often performed with local anesthesia |
| Before procedure | Increase fluids when instructed for local-anesthesia procedure |
| Expected afterward | Mild burning and pink-tinged urine |
| Unexpected afterward | Copious bleeding, large clots, inability to void, fever, or severe pain |

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
Pink urine can be expected after cystoscopy, but copious bleeding is not expected.
</div>
</div>

### Sterile Catheter Insertion

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Male Technique</strong>

- Hold the penis at a **90-degree angle** to the body.
- This straightens the urethra.
- Advance the catheter according to sterile procedure.
- Return the foreskin after insertion.
- Secure to the thigh or abdomen as directed.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Female Technique</strong>

- Separate the labia.
- Identify the urethra between the clitoris and vaginal opening.
- Cleanse front to back.
- Maintain visualization while inserting.
- Use a new sterile catheter if the vagina is entered.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Sterility</strong>

- Catheter insertion is sterile.
- Do not contaminate the catheter.
- Keep the sterile hand sterile.
- Use sterile gloves and supplies.
- Maintain a closed drainage system.
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Catheter Safety | Correct Action |
|---|---|
| Drainage bag | Keep below bladder level and off the floor |
| Tubing | Keep free of kinks and dependent loops |
| Male securement | Avoid pressure at the penile-scrotal junction |
| Closed system | Do not disconnect unnecessarily |
| Perineal care | Clean routinely and after contamination |
| Removal | Remove as soon as no longer needed |

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
Do not leave a male catheter pulling downward because constant pressure can damage the penile-scrotal junction.
</div>
</div>

### Urine Culture From an Indwelling Catheter

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Sampling Port Present</strong>

- Clamp the tubing below the sampling port.
- Allow fresh urine to collect.
- Cleanse the sampling port.
- Aspirate with a sterile syringe.
- Transfer to a sterile container.
- Unclamp the tubing.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>No Sampling Port</strong>

- Clamp tubing distal to the catheter bifurcation.
- Cleanse the catheter-tubing connection.
- Disconnect using sterile technique.
- Obtain the fresh sterile specimen.
- Reconnect and maintain drainage.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Never Use</strong>

- Do not collect a sterile culture from the drainage bag.
- Bag urine is old and contaminated.
- Do not break the closed system without correct sterile technique.
- Do not leave the tubing clamped.
</div>
</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Do Not Miss</h3>

A sterile urine culture comes from fresh urine at the sampling port or sterile catheter connection, never from the drainage bag.
</div>
</div>

<div class="mh-table-card" markdown="1">

<h3>Renal Diagnostics Big Picture</h3>

| Concept | Main Point | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **BUN** | Nitrogen waste | Know 10–20 mg/dL |
| **Creatinine** | Kidney filtration | Trend for worsening function |
| **IVP** | Iodine contrast imaging | Hold metformin 48 hours before and after |
| **Cystoscopy** | Scope of bladder | Pink urine expected; heavy bleeding is not |
| **Clean catch** | Midstream specimen | Clean, begin voiding, then collect |
| **Catheter culture** | Fresh sterile urine | Never collect from drainage bag |
| **Urine output** | Perfusion and filtration | Report below 30 mL/hr |

</div>

<div class="mh-columns" markdown="1">
<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Quick Review</h3>

1. BUN is 10–20 mg/dL.
2. Serum creatinine is 0.6–1.2 mg/dL.
3. Minimum urine output is 30 mL/hr.
4. A clean-catch specimen is collected in midstream.
5. Discard the first void of a 24-hour collection.
6. Hold metformin 48 hours before and after IV contrast.
7. Increase fluids after contrast when permitted.
8. Pink urine may occur after cystoscopy.
9. Catheter insertion is sterile.
10. Hold the penis at 90 degrees for male insertion.
11. Keep the drainage bag below the bladder.
12. Never collect a culture from the drainage bag.
</div>
<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Do Not Miss</h3>

- Do not touch the inside of a specimen cup.
- Do not keep a missed 24-hour urine collection secret.
- Do not give metformin around IV contrast in the course protocol.
- Do not ignore heavy bleeding after cystoscopy.
- Do not break catheter sterility.
- Do not allow dependent tubing loops.
- Do not collect a sterile culture from the drainage bag.
- Do not ignore urine output below 30 mL/hr.
</div>
</div>

# Renal Surgery & Urinary Diversions

<p class="mh-subtitle">ATI-focused review of postoperative renal surgery, respiratory and bleeding risks, urine output, ileal conduit, ureterosigmoidostomy, Indiana pouch, nephrostomy care, stoma assessment, and patient education</p>

Kidney surgery carries major **respiratory and bleeding risks** because the kidneys sit high in the abdomen and receive a large amount of cardiac output. Urinary diversions create a new route for urine after bladder removal or loss of bladder function.

### Postoperative Renal Surgery Priorities

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Respiratory Risk</strong>

- Kidneys sit high in the abdominal cavity.
- Incisional pain can limit deep breathing.
- Monitor respiratory rate and oxygen saturation.
- Assess breath sounds.
- Encourage pulmonary hygiene as allowed.
- Splint the incision.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Bleeding Risk</strong>

- Kidneys are extremely vascular.
- A large portion of cardiac output reaches the kidneys.
- Bleeding may be internal and not visible.
- Monitor H&H frequently.
- Watch heart rate, blood pressure, pallor, and swelling.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Urine Output</strong>

- Strict intake and output
- Absolute minimum **30 mL/hr**
- Desired course target **60 mL/hr**
- Check drainage tubes for kinks
- Report abrupt decrease
- Monitor color and bleeding
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Postoperative Finding | Nursing Response |
|---|---|
| Shallow breathing | Treat pain, position, splint, and encourage pulmonary hygiene |
| Tachycardia with falling BP | Assess possible internal bleeding |
| Falling H&H | Notify provider and evaluate blood loss |
| Urine below 30 mL/hr | Check tubing, perfusion, bleeding, and renal function |
| Bright red urinary bleeding | Assess amount and report significant bleeding |
| Flank or abdominal swelling | Assess for internal bleeding or obstruction |

</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- Respiratory distress
- Falling oxygen saturation
- Tachycardia with hypotension
- Falling H&H
- Rapid abdominal or flank swelling
- Urine output below 30 mL/hr
- No drainage from a urinary tube
</div>
</div>

### Ileal Conduit

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>What It Is</strong>

- Used after cystectomy or when bladder no longer functions.
- A segment of ileum forms a conduit.
- Ureters drain into the conduit.
- Urine exits through an abdominal stoma.
- An external pouch collects urine continuously.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Expected Stoma</strong>

- Pink or red
- Moist
- Mild swelling early after surgery
- Continuous urine drainage
- Small amount of blood immediately postoperatively may occur
- Mucus in urine is expected
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Unexpected Stoma</strong>

- Pale
- Dusky
- Blue
- Black
- Dry
- No urine drainage
- Severe bleeding
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Why Mucus Is Expected:</strong> The ileal conduit is made from intestinal tissue, so it continues producing mucus after becoming part of the urinary system.
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Ileal Conduit Care | Priority Action |
|---|---|
| Stoma assessment | Check color, moisture, size, and perfusion |
| Skin protection | Keep urine off peristomal skin |
| Appliance fit | Cut opening close to stoma size without constriction |
| Drainage | Empty pouch before it becomes heavy |
| Night drainage | Connect to larger drainage system as instructed |
| Urine | Monitor amount, color, odor, mucus, and signs of infection |

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
Mucus in ileal-conduit urine is expected. A dusky or black stoma is not expected.
</div>
</div>

### Ureterosigmoidostomy

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>What It Is</strong>

- Ureters are attached to the sigmoid colon.
- Urine enters the large bowel.
- The client eliminates urine through the rectum.
- Drainage resembles watery diarrhea.
- Nocturia and frequency are common.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Why Complications Occur</strong>

- Kidneys normally remove water.
- Large bowel normally reabsorbs water.
- Urine contacts bowel bacteria.
- Bacteria can ascend toward the kidneys.
- Fluid and electrolyte imbalance may develop.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Major Complications</strong>

- Infection
- Ascending kidney infection
- Fluid imbalance
- Electrolyte imbalance
- Frequent rectal elimination
- Lifestyle adjustment
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Assessment Area | Priority |
|---|---|
| Stool-like urinary drainage | Expected route after surgery |
| Fever or flank pain | Assess ascending infection |
| Dehydration | Monitor intake, output, weight, and electrolytes |
| Skin | Protect perianal skin from frequent watery drainage |
| Nocturia | Plan safety and access to bathroom |
| Psychosocial adjustment | Support coping with lifelong elimination changes |

</div>
</div>

### Indiana Pouch

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>What It Is</strong>

- Common continent urinary diversion
- Created after bladder removal or loss of function
- Uses a segment of ileum and cecum
- Forms an internal urine reservoir
- No continuously draining external pouch
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>How It Is Emptied</strong>

- Catheter inserted through the stoma
- Pouch drained at regular intervals
- Sterile or clean technique follows the care plan
- Emptying schedule must be followed
- Reservoir should not overfill
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Why Regular Emptying Matters</strong>

- Prevents waste reabsorption
- Prevents reflux to ureters
- Prevents urinary infection
- Protects pouch capacity
- Prevents leakage
- Protects kidney function
</div>
</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
The Indiana pouch is continent, but it is not self-emptying. The client must catheterize it regularly.
</div>
</div>

### Nephrostomy Tube Care

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Purpose</strong>

- Drains urine directly from the kidney
- Bypasses obstruction
- Protects kidney function
- May be temporary or long-term
- Exits through the flank or back
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Tube Safety</strong>

- Keep tubing patent.
- Prevent kinks.
- Keep drainage bag below kidney level.
- Secure tube.
- Never clamp unless specifically ordered.
- Maintain sterile dressing.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Report Immediately</strong>

- Abrupt decrease in drainage
- No drainage
- New flank pain
- Leakage around tube
- Fever
- Cloudy or foul urine
- Tube dislodgement
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Continuous urine drainage | Expected |
| Sudden output decrease with pain | Possible obstruction |
| Fever and cloudy drainage | Possible infection |
| Tube pulled outward | Possible dislodgement |
| Kinked tubing | Correct immediately to restore flow |
| Bag above kidney | Increases reflux and infection risk |

</div>
</div>

### Patient Teaching & Psychosocial Care

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Appliance Teaching</strong>

- Measure the stoma.
- Protect surrounding skin.
- Empty pouch before it is heavy.
- Carry extra supplies.
- Follow night-drainage instructions.
- Monitor for leaks.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Health Monitoring</strong>

- Track urine output.
- Report fever.
- Report flank pain.
- Report reduced drainage.
- Maintain hydration as instructed.
- Keep follow-up appointments.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Psychosocial Support</strong>

- Body-image concerns
- Sexuality concerns
- Fear of odor or leakage
- Social embarrassment
- Lifestyle adjustment
- Need for independence
- Ostomy support resources
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Diversion | Lifelong Skill |
|---|---|
| Ileal conduit | External pouch and skin care |
| Ureterosigmoidostomy | Rectal urinary elimination and fluid monitoring |
| Indiana pouch | Scheduled catheterization |
| Nephrostomy | Tube, dressing, drainage, and obstruction monitoring |

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Red and Moist Is Right:</strong> A healthy urinary stoma is pink or red and moist, while pale, dusky, or black requires immediate attention.
</div>
</div>

<div class="mh-table-card" markdown="1">

<h3>Renal Surgery & Diversions Big Picture</h3>

| Concept | Main Point | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Renal surgery** | High abdominal and highly vascular | Respiratory care and bleeding surveillance |
| **Urine output** | Reflects renal perfusion and drainage | Minimum 30 mL/hr |
| **Ileal conduit** | Continuous external drainage | Healthy stoma and skin protection |
| **Mucus** | Intestinal tissue still secretes mucus | Expected with ileal conduit |
| **Ureterosigmoidostomy** | Urine drains through rectum | Infection and fluid-electrolyte monitoring |

</div>

<div class="mh-columns" markdown="1">
<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Quick Review</h3>

1. Renal surgery carries respiratory and bleeding risks.
2. Monitor H&H because bleeding may be internal.
3. Minimum urine output is 30 mL/hr.
4. Desired postoperative course target is 60 mL/hr.
5. An ileal conduit drains continuously into an external pouch.
6. Mucus is expected because the conduit is intestinal tissue.
7. A healthy stoma is pink or red and moist.
8. Ureterosigmoidostomy causes urinary elimination through the rectum.
9. Ureterosigmoidostomy increases infection and fluid-electrolyte risks.
10. An Indiana pouch must be catheterized regularly.
11. Keep nephrostomy tubing patent and below kidney level.
12. Never clamp a nephrostomy tube unless ordered.
</div>
<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Do Not Miss</h3>

- Do not overlook shallow breathing after kidney surgery.
- Do not wait for visible blood to assess internal bleeding.
- Do not ignore urine below 30 mL/hr.
- Do not call mucus in ileal-conduit urine infection by itself.
- Do not ignore a dusky or black stoma.
- Do not allow an Indiana pouch to overfill.
- Do not kink or clamp a nephrostomy tube.
- Do not overlook fever or flank pain with a diversion.
</div>
</div>

<div class="cheer-banner">
  Renal surgery questions usually come down to breathing, bleeding, urine output, stoma perfusion, drainage patency, and infection.
</div>

# NCLEX Quick Cues: Renal & Urinary

<div class="priority-focus">
  <div>
    <h3>Think First</h3>
    Urine Output • Potassium • Fluid Volume • Creatinine • Obstruction • Infection • Dialysis Access • Respiratory Status
  </div>
</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Urine Output Cue</strong>

- Less than **30 mL/hr** is urgent.
- First check tubing, kinks, clamps, bladder distention, perfusion, and obstruction.
- Abrupt anuria is more concerning than a stable low baseline.
- After renal surgery, the course target is often **60 mL/hr**.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Potassium Cue</strong>

- Hyperkalemia is the most dangerous renal electrolyte problem.
- Peaked T waves or dysrhythmias require immediate action.
- Oliguric clients often need potassium restriction.
- Dialysis may be required when potassium does not respond to treatment.
</div>

<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1">
<strong>Fluid Cue</strong>

- Daily weight is the best routine indicator of fluid change.
- One kilogram is approximately one liter of fluid.
- Crackles, edema, hypertension, and rapid weight gain suggest overload.
- Hypotension and dizziness after dialysis suggest excess fluid removal.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| When You See This | Think This | Best Nursing Move |
|---|---|---|
| **Flank pain + fever + CVA tenderness** | Pyelonephritis | Obtain culture, begin prescribed treatment, and watch for sepsis |
| **Dysuria + urgency + frequency** | Lower UTI | Collect clean-catch urine before the first antimicrobial dose when possible |
| **Recent strep throat + hematuria + edema + hypertension** | Acute glomerulonephritis | Strict I&O, daily weight, low sodium, and monitor potassium |
| **Severe wave-like pain radiating to groin** | Ureteral stone | Pain control, fluids if not contraindicated, strain urine, and monitor obstruction |
| **No urine + bladder distention** | Lower urinary obstruction | Check catheter patency or assess for retention before assuming kidney failure |
| **No urine + flank pain after stone history** | Upper urinary obstruction | Report promptly because hydronephrosis and kidney damage can occur |
| **Cloudy peritoneal dialysate** | Peritonitis | Stop and report according to protocol; maintain sterile technique |
| **No thrill or bruit in AV fistula** | Clotted access | Do not use the access and notify the provider immediately |
| **Headache + confusion + seizure after dialysis** | Disequilibrium syndrome | Protect airway and safety and report immediately |
| **Bright red urine + large clots after TURP** | Excessive bleeding or obstruction | Check drainage and irrigation, then report significant bleeding |
| **Dusky or black urinary stoma** | Impaired perfusion | Report immediately |
| **Mucus in ileal-conduit urine** | Expected intestinal secretion | Continue routine care unless other infection signs are present |

</div>

<div class="mh-columns" markdown="1">

<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Priority Order</h3>

1. Airway and breathing if pulmonary edema or severe fluid overload is present.
2. Cardiac rhythm if potassium is elevated.
3. Restore urine flow if obstruction is suspected.
4. Treat infection and sepsis risk.
5. Protect dialysis access.
6. Manage pain, nutrition, skin, and long-term teaching.
</div>

<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Fast Traps</h3>

- Do not collect a sterile catheter culture from the drainage bag.
- Do not use a dialysis-access arm for blood pressure, IVs, or venipuncture.
- Do not ignore cloudy peritoneal effluent.
- Do not confuse expected mucus with infection in an ileal conduit.
- Do not calculate TURP urine output without subtracting irrigation.
- Do not give unrestricted potassium to an oliguric client.
</div>

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Renal NCLEX Rule:</strong> Check urine flow, potassium, fluid status, infection, and access patency before anything less urgent.
</div>

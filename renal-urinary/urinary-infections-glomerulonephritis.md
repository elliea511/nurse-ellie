# Urinary Infections & Glomerulonephritis

<p class="mh-subtitle">ATI-focused review of cystitis, pyelonephritis, clean-catch cultures, urinary medications, infection prevention, acute glomerulonephritis, edema, hypertension, and nursing priorities</p>

A **lower urinary tract infection** mainly affects the bladder and causes frequency, urgency, dysuria, and suprapubic pain. **Pyelonephritis** affects the kidney and causes fever, flank pain, and systemic illness. **Acute glomerulonephritis** is an inflammatory glomerular disorder commonly linked in the course notes to a recent streptococcal throat infection.

### Cystitis

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Classic Findings</strong>

- Frequency
- Urgency
- Dysuria
- Suprapubic tenderness
- Bladder pain
- Pyuria
- Hematuria
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>First Priority</strong>

- Obtain a clean-catch urine specimen.
- Culture before antimicrobial therapy when possible.
- Urinalysis may suggest infection before culture results return.
- Begin prescribed treatment promptly.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Why Women Are More Prone</strong>

- Shorter urethra
- Urinary meatus closer to rectal organisms
- Sexual activity
- Pregnancy
- Incomplete bladder emptying
- Improper wiping
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | Urinating often |
| **Urgency** | Sudden strong need to urinate |
| **Dysuria** | Pain or burning with urination |
| **Pyuria** | Pus or white cells in urine |
| **Hematuria** | Blood in urine |
| **Suprapubic pain** | Pain over the bladder |

</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
The first action in a stable client with UTI symptoms is usually obtaining the urine specimen before antimicrobial treatment.
</div>
</div>

### Pyelonephritis

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Upper UTI Findings</strong>

- Fever
- Chills
- Flank pain
- Costovertebral angle tenderness
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Malaise
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Urinary Findings</strong>

- Dysuria
- Frequency
- Urgency
- Pyuria
- Hematuria
- Cloudy urine
- Positive culture
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Major Complications</strong>

- Sepsis
- Recurrent kidney infection
- Renal scarring
- Chronic kidney damage
- Abscess
- Dehydration
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Upper Versus Lower:</strong> Bladder symptoms are lower UTI. Fever, flank pain, and CVA tenderness mean the infection has reached the kidney.
</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- Hypotension
- Confusion
- Tachycardia
- Reduced urine output
- High fever
- Persistent vomiting
- Worsening flank pain
</div>
</div>

### UTI Medications

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Medication | Main Use | Priority Teaching |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenazopyridine or Pyridium** | Urinary tract analgesic | Relieves burning within about 30 minutes in the course notes and turns urine bright orange |
| **Sulfisoxazole or Gantrisin** | Sulfonamide antimicrobial | Increase fluids and avoid excessive sun |
| **Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or Bactrim** | UTI antimicrobial | Complete the prescription and monitor sulfa allergy |
| **Nitrofurantoin or Macrodantin** | Urinary anti-infective | Used primarily for UTI and works best in acidic urine in the course notes |
| **Amoxicillin** | Antimicrobial when organism is sensitive | Complete the full prescription and monitor allergy |

</div>

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Phenazopyridine</strong>

- Relieves urinary pain.
- Does not kill the organism.
- Gives comfort while the antimicrobial treats the infection.
- Turns urine bright orange.
- May stain clothing.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Sulfonamide Safety</strong>

- Increase fluids.
- Avoid excessive sunlight.
- Use sunscreen.
- Report rash.
- Monitor kidney-stone symptoms.
- Complete the prescription.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Urinary Anti-Infectives</strong>

- Methenamine mandelate
- Nalidixic acid
- Nitrofurantoin
- Acidic urine may improve effectiveness in course notes.
- Cranberry juice may acidify urine.
- Obtain cultures before and after therapy as ordered.
</div>
</div>

<div class="nclex-tip" markdown="1">
Orange urine from phenazopyridine is expected and does not mean the infection is worsening.
</div>
</div>

### UTI Prevention & Catheter Safety

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Daily Prevention</strong>

- Wipe front to back.
- Increase fluids when permitted.
- Do not delay urination.
- Empty the bladder completely.
- Avoid irritating feminine products.
- Wear breathable underwear.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Sexual Activity Teaching</strong>

- Void after intercourse.
- Clean the genital area gently.
- Avoid spermicides if they trigger recurrent infection.
- Report recurrent symptoms.
- Maintain hydration.
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Catheter-Associated UTI Prevention</strong>

- Avoid unnecessary catheters.
- Maintain a closed system.
- Keep the bag below the bladder.
- Prevent kinks and dependent loops.
- Perform routine perineal care.
- Remove as soon as possible.
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Finding | Follow-Up |
|---|---|
| Symptoms improve | Complete all medication |
| Symptoms persist | Notify provider and review culture |
| Fever or flank pain develops | Evaluate for pyelonephritis |
| Confusion in older adult | Assess for infection and systemic illness |
| Urine output falls | Assess obstruction, hydration, and renal function |

</div>
</div>

### Acute Glomerulonephritis

<div class="detail-grid" markdown="1">
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Classic History Cue</strong>

- Recent beta-hemolytic streptococcal throat infection
- More common in children because they have more streptococcal infections
- Immune complexes damage the glomerular membrane
- Blood and protein leak into urine
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Acute Edematous Phase</strong>

- Hematuria
- Proteinuria
- Increased urine specific gravity
- Edema
- Hypertension
- Fever
- Lethargy
</div>
<div class="detail-mini-card" markdown="1"><strong>Recovery or Diuretic Phase</strong>

- Diuresis
- Urine output increases
- Retained fluid is excreted
- Edema decreases
- Blood pressure may improve
- Monitor dehydration and electrolyte loss
</div>
</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Glomerular Injury:</strong> Antibodies damage the glomerular basement membrane → blood and protein enter the urine → filtration decreases → sodium and water are retained → edema and hypertension develop.
</div>

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Nursing Priority | Intervention |
|---|---|
| **Fluid balance** | Strict intake and output and daily weights |
| **Hypertension** | Frequent vital signs and prescribed antihypertensives |
| **Edema** | Low-sodium diet and respiratory assessment |
| **Oliguria** | Low-potassium diet because kidneys excrete most daily potassium |
| **Urine changes** | Monitor hematuria, proteinuria, and specific gravity |
| **Activity** | Rest during the acute phase |
| **Prognosis** | Course notes emphasize about a 90% cure rate when treated promptly |

</div>

<div class="report-now" markdown="1">
<h3>Report Immediately</h3>

- New crackles or respiratory distress
- Severe hypertension
- Rapid weight gain
- Falling urine output
- Hyperkalemia
- Confusion or seizure
</div>
</div>

### Lower UTI vs. Pyelonephritis vs. Glomerulonephritis

<div class="detail-table" markdown="1">

| Feature | Cystitis | Pyelonephritis | Acute Glomerulonephritis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main site | Bladder | Kidney infection | Glomerular inflammation |
| Major history | Urinary infection risks | Lower UTI may ascend | Recent streptococcal throat infection |
| Pain | Suprapubic | Flank and CVA | May have generalized discomfort and edema |
| Fever | May be absent or mild | Common | May occur |
| Urine | Pyuria and hematuria | Pyuria, hematuria, infection findings | Hematuria and proteinuria |
| Blood pressure | Usually not major feature | May change with systemic illness | Hypertension is a major finding |
| Edema | Not expected | Not primary | Major finding |
| Priority | Culture and antimicrobial treatment | Prevent sepsis and kidney damage | Control fluid, blood pressure, and potassium |

</div>

<div class="detail-callout" markdown="1">
<strong>Bladder Burns, Kidney Infection Hurts the Flank, Glomeruli Hold Fluid:</strong> Cystitis causes dysuria, pyelonephritis causes fever and flank pain, and glomerulonephritis causes hematuria, edema, and hypertension.
</div>
</div>

<div class="mh-table-card" markdown="1">

<h3>Urinary Infection & Glomerulonephritis Big Picture</h3>

| Concept | Main Point | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Cystitis** | Lower urinary infection | Culture before treatment |
| **Pyelonephritis** | Kidney infection | Watch for sepsis |
| **Phenazopyridine** | Urinary analgesic | Orange urine is expected |
| **Sulfonamides** | UTI antimicrobials | Fluids and sun protection |
| **Acute glomerulonephritis** | Post-strep glomerular inflammation | Monitor edema, BP, urine, and potassium |
| **Oliguria** | Reduced urine output | Restrict potassium as ordered |
| **Daily weight** | Fluid-balance indicator | Report rapid gain |

</div>

<div class="mh-columns" markdown="1">
<div class="quick-review" markdown="1">
<h3>Quick Review</h3>

1. Cystitis causes frequency, urgency, dysuria, and suprapubic pain.
2. Obtain a clean-catch urine before antimicrobial treatment when possible.
3. Phenazopyridine turns urine orange.
4. Sulfonamides require fluids and sun protection.
5. Pyelonephritis causes fever, flank pain, and CVA tenderness.
6. Pyelonephritis can progress to sepsis.
7. Recent strep throat is the classic acute-glomerulonephritis cue.
8. Acute glomerulonephritis causes hematuria and proteinuria.
9. Edema and hypertension result from fluid retention.
10. Use strict intake and output and daily weights.
11. Use a low-sodium diet.
12. Use a low-potassium diet when oliguric and prescribed.
</div>
<div class="do-not-miss" markdown="1">
<h3>Do Not Miss</h3>

- Do not give the first antimicrobial dose before a required culture when possible.
- Do not confuse orange urine with bleeding when the client takes phenazopyridine.
- Do not ignore fever and flank pain.
- Do not overlook confusion or hypotension as possible sepsis.
- Do not miss recent strep throat in glomerulonephritis.
- Do not ignore rapid weight gain or crackles.
- Do not give unrestricted potassium to an oliguric client.
</div>
</div>

<div class="cheer-banner">
  Infection questions focus on culture and progression. Glomerulonephritis questions focus on post-strep history, hematuria, edema, hypertension, and oliguria.
</div>
